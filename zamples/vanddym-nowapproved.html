INDICATION
[BRAND NAME] (anitocabtagene autoleucel) is a D-Domain-based B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA)-directed genetically modified autologous T-cell immunotherapy indicated for the treatment of:
• Adult patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma after three or more prior therapies including a proteasome inhibitor (PI), an immunomodulatory agent (IMiD), and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody.
This indication is approved under accelerated approval based on response rate. Continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in confirmatory trial(s).
IMPORTANT SAFETY INFORMATION
WARNING: CYTOKINE RELEASE SYNDROME, IMMUNE EFFECTOR CELL-ASSOCIATED NEUROTOXICITY SYNDROME, and SECONDARY HEMATOLOGICAL MALIGNANCIES
• Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS), which may be fatal or life-threatening, occurred in patients receiving VANDDMYO. Do not administer VANDDMYO to patients with active infection or inflammatory disorders. Treat CRS with tocilizumab and corticosteroids.
• Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS), which may be severe, occurred in patients receiving VANDDMYO, including concurrently with CRS, after CRS resolution or in the absence of CRS. Monitor for neurologic events after treatment with VANDDMYO. Provide supportive care and/or corticosteroids, as needed.
• T cell malignancies have occurred following treatment of hematologic malignancies with BCMA- and CD19-directed genetically modified autologous T cell immunotherapies.

WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
CYTOKINE RELEASE SYNDROME (CRS)
Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS) reactions occurred following treatment with [BRAND NAME]. CRS (ASTCT 2019) occurred in 85% (100/117) of patients, including ≥ Grade 3 CRS in 1% (1/117) of patients. The median time to onset of CRS, any grade, was 3.5 days (range: 1 to 17 days). CRS resolved in 99% of patients with a median duration of 2 days (range: 1 to 9 days). Ninety-five percent (95%) of patients had either no CRS or CRS that resolved within 10 days from infusion. The most common (≥ 10%) manifestations of CRS included pyrexia and hypotension. Severe events that may be associated with CRS include hypotension, febrile neutropenia, pyrexia, and hypoxia.
Identify CRS based on clinical presentation. Evaluate for and treat other causes of fever, hypoxia, and hypotension such as infection. CRS has been reported to be associated with findings of Immune Effector Cell-associated Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis-like Syndrome (IEC-HS) and Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH), and the physiology of the syndromes may overlap. IEC-HS/HLH is a potentially life-threatening condition. In patients with progressive symptoms of CRS or refractory CRS despite treatment, evaluate for evidence of IEC-HS/HLH.
Confirm tocilizumab and emergency equipment are available prior to thawing of [BRAND NAME].
Of the 117 patients who received [BRAND NAME] in iMMagine-1, 78% (91/117) of patients received tocilizumab for CRS. Seventy-six percent (76%) (89/117) of patients received at least 1 dose of corticosteroids for CRS. Seventy-five percent (75%) (88/117) of patients received both corticosteroids and tocilizumab for CRS.
Monitor patients at least daily for 7 days following [BRAND NAME] infusion for signs and symptoms of CRS. At the first sign of CRS, immediately institute treatment with supportive care, tocilizumab and corticosteroids, as indicated in Table 1 of the US PRESCRIBING INFORMATION. Counsel patients to seek immediate medical attention should signs or symptoms of CRS occur at any time.
IMMUNE EFFECTOR CELL-ASSOCIATED NEUROTOXICITY SYNDROME (ICANS)
Immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), which may be severe, has occurred following treatment with [BRAND NAME].
In patients receiving [BRAND NAME] in the iMMagine-1 study, ICANS occurred in 8% (9/117) of patients, including Grade 3 in 1% (1/117) and no Grade 4 or 5 events. The median time to onset of ICANS was 7 days (range: 2 to 31 days). The median duration of ICANS was 6 days (range: 2 to 29 days) in all patients. ICANS resolved in all 9 patients and median time to resolution was 10 days (range: 7 to 36 days) from infusion. After day 31 from infusion, no new instances of ICANS were observed and after day 36, all ICANS were resolved. Of patients with ICANS, 78% (7/9) had CRS. The onset of ICANS occurred concurrent with CRS in 1 patient, and after the onset of CRS in 6 patients. The most frequent (≥ 5%) manifestation of ICANS was confusional state.
Monitor patients at least daily for 7 days following infusion for signs and symptoms of neurologic toxicities. Rule out other causes of neurologic symptoms. Continue to monitor patients for signs or symptoms of neurologic toxicities for at least two weeks after infusion and treat promptly. Advise patients to avoid driving for at least 3 days following resolution of all treatment-related neurologic symptoms.
INFECTIONS
[BRAND NAME] should not be administered to patients with active infection or inflammatory disorders. Severe, life-threatening, or fatal infections occurred in patients after [BRAND NAME] infusion.
Among patients receiving [BRAND NAME] in the iMMagine-1 study, infections (all grade) occurred in 56% (65/117), with Grade 3 or higher infections occurring in 9% of patients. Grade 3 or higher infections with an unspecified pathogen occurred in 1%, viral infections in 3%, bacterial infections in 1%, and fungal infections in 2% of patients. Overall, 1% (1/117) of patients had a Grade 5 infection. [BRAND NAME] should not be administered to patients with clinically significant active systemic infections.
Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of infection before and after [BRAND NAME] infusion and treat patients appropriately. Administer prophylactic, pre-emptive and/or therapeutic antimicrobials (including broad-spectrum antifungals for mucormycosis) according to local guidelines.
Febrile neutropenia was observed in 3% of patients after [BRAND NAME] infusion and may be concurrent with CRS. In the event of febrile neutropenia, evaluate for infection and manage with broad-spectrum antibiotics, fluids and/or other supportive care, as medically indicated.
Viral Reactivation
Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation, in some cases resulting in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure and death, can occur in patients with hypogammaglobulinemia.
Perform screening for cytomegalovirus (CMV), HBV, hepatitis C virus (HCV), and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) or any other infectious agents if clinically indicated in accordance with clinical guidelines before collection of cells for manufacturing. Consider antiviral therapy to prevent viral reactivation per local institutional guidelines/clinical practice.
PROLONGED CYTOPENIAS
Patients may exhibit prolonged cytopenias following lymphodepleting chemotherapy and [BRAND NAME] infusion.
Among patients receiving [BRAND NAME] in the iMMagine-1 study, Grade 3 or higher cytopenias not resolved by day 28 following [BRAND NAME] infusion occurred in 49% (57/117) of patients and included neutropenia in 35% (41/117) and thrombocytopenia in 30% (35/117). In all (41/41) patients who recovered from Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia after day 28 from [BRAND NAME] infusion, the median time to recovery was 1.1 months. In 91% (32/35) of patients who recovered from Grade 3 or 4 thrombocytopenia, the median time to recovery was 1.1 months.
Monitor blood counts prior to and after [BRAND NAME] infusion. Manage cytopenias with growth factors and blood product transfusion support according to local institutional guidelines.
HYPOGAMMAGLOBULINEMIA
Hypogammaglobulinemia can occur in patients receiving treatment with [BRAND NAME].
Hypogammaglobulinemia occurred in 43% of patients. Seventy-one percent (83/117) of patients received intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) post [BRAND NAME] for either an adverse reaction or prophylaxis.
Monitor immunoglobulin levels after treatment with [BRAND NAME] and administer IVIG for IgG <400 mg/dL. Manage per local institutional guidelines, including infection precautions and antibiotic or antiviral prophylaxis.
Use of Live Vaccines
The safety of immunization with live viral vaccines during or following [BRAND NAME] treatment has not been studied. Vaccination with live virus vaccines is not recommended for at least 6 weeks prior to the start of lymphodepleting chemotherapy, during [BRAND NAME] treatment, and until immune recovery following treatment with [BRAND NAME].
IMMUNE EFFECTOR CELL-ASSOCIATED HEMOPHAGOCYTIC LYMPHOHISTIOCYTOSIS-LIKE SYNDROME (IEC-HS) AND HEMOPHAGOCYTIC LYMPHOHISTIOCYTOSIS (HLH)
Among patients receiving [BRAND NAME] in the iMMagine-1 study, HLH occurred in 1% (1/117) and IEC-HS occurred in 3% (3/117). HLH median onset was day 5 and IEC-HS median onset was 7 days (range: 6 to 8 days).
IEC-HS and HLH are potentially life-threatening conditions with a high mortality rate if not recognized early and treated. Treatment of IEC-HS and HLH should be administered per institutional guidelines.
HYPERSENSITIVITY REACTIONS
Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur due to dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in [BRAND NAME].
SECONDARY MALIGNANCIES
Patients treated with [BRAND NAME] may develop secondary malignancies of T cell origin. T cell malignancies have occurred following treatment of hematologic malignancies with BCMA- and CD19-directed genetically modified autologous T cell immunotherapies. Mature T cell malignancies, including CAR-positive tumors, may present as soon as weeks following infusion, and may include fatal outcomes.
Monitor lifelong for secondary malignancies of T cell origin. In the event that a secondary malignancy of T cell origin occurs, contact Kite at 1-844-454-KITE (5483) for reporting and to obtain instructions on patient samples to collect for testing.
ADVERSE REACTIONS
The most common non-laboratory adverse reactions (incidence ≥ 20%) in patients treated with [BRAND NAME] in the iMMagine-1 Study included CRS, hypogammaglobulinemia, fatigue, nausea, headache, upper respiratory tract infection, viral infection, and musculoskeletal pain.
The most common laboratory abnormalities (incidence ≥ 50%) in patients treated with [BRAND NAME] in the iMMagine-1 Study included l leukocytes decreased, neutrophils decreased, and lymphocytes decreased.
